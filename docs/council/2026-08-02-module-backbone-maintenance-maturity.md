# Council — module:backbone-maintenance — focus: MATURITY

## Best call
**Verdict: NOT complete.** First and load-bearing move — **close the invariant-bypass on the delivered visit surface now**: drop `status`, `parts_cost`, `total_cost`, `journal_id`, `accounting_post_id` from `PatchMaintenanceVisitDto` (`src/presentation/dto/maintenance_visit_dto.rs:143-183`) and stop mounting generic mutable CRUD on `MaintenanceVisit` via `all_crud_routes()` (`src/lib.rs:77-88`); ship `create_readonly_maintenance_routes` as the only prod visit path. This is the domain-expert's smallest model change, and it is the one move every seat agrees is necessary regardless of which way the bigger "wire-it-in vs. re-scope" question is later resolved.

Why this and not "wire the engine in" (yagni/steelman) as the first move: wiring naively publishes the engine's *concrete* ports as the public contract — exactly the "leaked internal as integration point" anti-pattern the contract-seat flags — and that contract-shape decision benefits from a real consumer that does not exist (verified: zero workspace refs). Closing the bypass is separable, cheaper, needs no consumer, and stops the only ACTIVE harm. It makes the maturity verdict honest without committing to a blind contract.

- **Residual negative value:** the completion engine stays unreachable through the module's public API — the module ships a SAFE surface, not a COMPLETE one. Concretely: a sophisticated, tested, idempotent, balanced, RLS-fenced engine (`maintenance_write_service.rs:175-305`) remains callable only by the module's own tests. The residual is unrealized value (locked engine, roughly one day of connective tissue away from shipping), NOT destroyed value — and importantly, no longer a silent-correctness hole. The forbidden transition (`PATCH {status:"completed", total_cost:0}` with no inventory issue and no journal) is removed from the shipped API.
- **Reversibility:** easy. No workspace consumer depends on the mutable PATCH shape or on `all_crud_routes()` for the visit (verified by workspace-wide grep). DTO field-set changes and route-composition changes are trivially re-addable.
- **What would flip this:** (1) discovery of an existing downstream `backend-service` that already depends on the mutable PATCH field set — closure would become a breaking change; but the verified facts show none exists, so this is hypothetical. OR (2) a team decision that this module is intentionally engine-only — in which case the call flips to "re-scope and document as engine-only, and fold the bypass closure into that re-scope" (recommendation #4 below). The single fact that would change everything: a named, in-flight consumer with a pinned contract expectation.

## Disagreement map
The real tensions. For each: the crux, and who is on each side.
- **Verdict: FALSE-and-harmful vs. TRUE-but-unactualized** — Skeptic says "complete = FALSE; the delivered surface (`all_crud_routes` PATCH) IS the invariant-bypass vector, so it's worse than unactualized." Steelman says "complete-on-the-module-bar IF (C1) a consumer exists; boundary design is correct, just unactualized," with the honesty qualifier that no consumer can be located. Crux: do "zero consumers" + "shipped surface is bypassable" make it FALSE, or merely unactualized? The verified facts decide it — Steelman's load-bearing C1 is factually false (empty `// <<< CUSTOM` blocks at `lib.rs:62-63,140-141,147-148`; `MaintenanceWriteService` is not a field, not built, not re-exported, not routed; the bypass PATCH IS mounted), and Steelman's own qualifier concedes the collapse. **Verdict sides with Skeptic.**
- **Move: add-the-correct-path vs. remove-the-wrong-path** — yagni says "wire the cheap connective tissue now (~1 day); the work that makes it ship is exactly what's missing." Contract-seat says "don't publish internals blind — the engine's concrete ports would become the integration point; pick a trait abstraction first." Crux: is the connective tissue genuinely cheap, or does it require a consumer-validating contract-design step? It splits — the SAFETY half (close bypass) is cheap and separable; the CONTRACT half (publish engine trait) is committing and benefits from a real consumer. **Move sides with the safety half now; contract half deferred to recommendation #2/#3.**
- **Contract orientation: engine-as-promise vs. engine-as-internal** — contract-seat offers it as either/or: (a) engine is the contract → publish a trait + implement `MaintenanceQueryService` over read repos; or (b) engine is internal → delete the unbacked `MaintenanceQueryService` advertisement (`exports/services.rs:23`, impl removed in the diff) and document as engine+tests only. Crux: is there a near-term consumer? Unresolved by the seats. The best call does NOT resolve it — it keeps both futures open by making the current surface safe either way.
